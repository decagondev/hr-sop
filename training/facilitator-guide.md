# Facilitator guide — Hire for an open role: requisition to signed contract

A guide for whoever is *teaching* this SOP to a new operator. Aim for a 45–60 min session: 10 min context, 30 min walk-through, the rest practice.

## Before the session

Make sure each learner has: ATS with permission to create requisitions and post roles, Approved offer-letter and employment-agreement templates, Background-screening (CRA) account with disclosure/authorization forms, ats, cra-api, e-signature, e-verify.
Confirm they can reach every tool listed in the SOP prerequisites.

## Learning objectives

By the end, a learner can:
- A qualified candidate hired through a consistent, documented, defensible process
- A fully executed employment agreement on file
- Right-to-work verification initiated with a clean handoff to onboarding

## Walk-through — talking points per step

**1. S1 — Open and document the requisition** (Hiring Manager)
  - What it is: Capture the confirmed hiring need as a requisition in the ATS: role, level, reporting line, justification, target start, and the business case. This is the record everything else hangs off.
  - How they'll know it worked: A requisition record exists in the ATS with role, level, reporting line, and business case.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S1?”

**2. S2 — Approve headcount and budget** (Compensation / Finance Approver)
  - What it is: Finance / compensation reviews the requisition against headcount plan and budget and either approves it to open or returns it. No sourcing happens before approval.
  - How they'll know it worked: The requisition shows an approval record with approver, date, and budgeted band.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S2?”

**3. S3 — Finalize the job description and selection criteria** (Recruiter / Talent Acquisition)
  - What it is: With the hiring manager, turn the requisition into an approved job description and a structured scorecard: 6–12 job-related competencies, each with behavioral anchors. Requirements must be necessary for the role.
  - How they'll know it worked: Every listed requirement is job-related and consistent with business necessity; the scorecard defines competencies with behavioral anchors.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S3?”

**4. S4 — Source candidates** (Recruiter / Talent Acquisition)
  - What it is: Post the role to the required internal and external channels and run sourcing and referrals to build a diverse, qualified pipeline. Honor any internal-posting or pay-transparency requirements.
  - How they'll know it worked: The role is posted to all required channels and a candidate pipeline is building in the ATS.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S4?”

**5. S5 — Screen applications** (Recruiter / Talent Acquisition)
  - What it is: Review applicants against the same job-related criteria and shortlist. Do not ask about criminal history at this stage where fair-chance law applies.
  - How they'll know it worked: All applicants are screened against the same criteria and screening decisions are logged.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S5?”

**6. S6 — Recruiter phone screen** (Recruiter / Talent Acquisition)
  - What it is: Hold a consistent phone/video screen with each shortlisted candidate to confirm fit, motivation, logistics, and compensation expectations.
  - How they'll know it worked: Each candidate is screened with the same core questions; notes are recorded in the ATS.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S6?”

**7. S7 — Administer job-related assessments** (Recruiter / Talent Acquisition)
  - What it is: Where the role calls for it, administer the same validated, job-related assessment to every candidate and record results. Skip only if no assessment is used for this role.
  - How they'll know it worked: The same assessment is sent to all candidates for the role and results are recorded.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S7?”

**8. S8 — Conduct structured interviews** (Interview Panel)
  - What it is: Run structured panel interviews using the same core questions for every candidate, scoring against the BARS scorecard. Offer reasonable accommodations on request.
  - How they'll know it worked: Every interviewer completes an anchored scorecard with evidence notes; the same core questions were used.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S8?”

**9. S9 — Debrief and make the selection decision** (Hiring Manager)
  - What it is: Convene a structured debrief to reconcile scores against the anchors — not to reach consensus by social pressure — and make a documented, job-related decision. Retain the records.
  - How they'll know it worked: The decision and its rationale are documented and stored; the selection-rate is checked against the 4/5 rule.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S9?”

**10. S10 — Complete reference checks** (Recruiter / Talent Acquisition)
  - What it is: Run consistent, job-related reference checks on the selected candidate and record the results before making the offer conditional.
  - How they'll know it worked: References are completed with consistent questions and results are recorded.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S10?”

**11. S11 — Approve compensation and extend a conditional verbal offer** (Recruiter / Talent Acquisition)
  - What it is: Confirm the package is approved and within band, then extend a verbal offer that is explicitly conditional on clearing screening and right-to-work checks. Follow up in writing summarizing the key terms.
  - How they'll know it worked: Compensation is approved and within band; the conditional verbal offer and its terms are confirmed by email.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S11?”

**12. S12 — Candidate response and negotiation** (Candidate)
  - What it is: The candidate accepts, negotiates, or declines the conditional offer. Route accordingly; re-approve compensation for any counter-offer.
  - How they'll know it worked: The candidate's response is recorded; any negotiated changes are captured before proceeding.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S12?”

**13. S13 — Disclose, authorize, and run the background check** (HR Business Partner)
  - What it is: Provide the standalone FCRA disclosure and obtain separate written authorization, then order the background check via the CRA. Keep the conditional offer in place while screening runs.
  - How they'll know it worked: A signed standalone disclosure and authorization are on file before the report is ordered; the report is stored securely.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S13?”

**14. S14 — Adjudicate the results** (HR Business Partner)
  - What it is: Review the background and reference results. If nothing disqualifying is found, proceed to the written offer. If there is potentially disqualifying information, apply an individualized assessment and enter adverse action.
  - How they'll know it worked: The adjudication applies job-relatedness and an individualized assessment; the rationale is documented.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S14?”

**15. S15 — Run FCRA adverse action (only if withdrawing)** (HR Business Partner)
  - What it is: Send the pre-adverse-action notice with a copy of the report and the Summary of Rights, wait a reasonable period for dispute (best practice at least five business days; some jurisdictions longer), then either restore the offer or send the final adverse-action notice.
  - How they'll know it worked: Both notices are documented with dates and the waiting period is observed; no irreversible internal action is taken during the waiting period.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S15?”

**16. S16 — Extend the formal written offer** (HR Business Partner)
  - What it is: Issue the written offer letter / employment agreement matching the approved compensation and the verbal terms, with an acceptance deadline. Route any non-standard terms to Legal first.
  - How they'll know it worked: The sent offer matches approved terms; non-standard terms were reviewed by Legal; an acceptance deadline is set.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S16?”

**17. S17 — Countersign and execute the agreement** (HR Business Partner)
  - What it is: On candidate signature, countersign to fully execute the employment agreement and confirm the start date. This is the signed contract that completes the hire.
  - How they'll know it worked: Candidate signature and employer countersignature are on file; the start date is confirmed.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S17?”

**18. S18 — Initiate right-to-work and hand off to onboarding** (HR Business Partner)
  - What it is: Start Form I-9 and (if enrolled) E-Verify, and hand the new hire to onboarding. Section 1 is due no later than the first day; Section 2 within three business days of the start date.
  - How they'll know it worked: Form I-9 is initiated and the onboarding handoff package is delivered to the onboarding owner.
  - Ask the learner: “What would you check before moving on from S18?”

## Sign-off
